# 043 - Soar: An Architecture for General Intelligence (1987)

**Authors:** John E. Laird, Allen Newell, Paul S. Rosenbloom

**Source:** Artificial Intelligence, Volume 33, Issues 1-2, Pages 1-64

## Summary

This foundational paper presents SOAR, a cognitive architecture designed as an implemented proposal for general intelligence. The authors articulate the goal of providing the underlying structure that would enable a system to perform the full range of cognitive tasks, employ the full range of problem-solving methods and representations, and learn about all aspects of tasks and its performance. SOAR is built on the principle that intelligence requires a single unified architecture with a minimal set of mechanisms rather than many specialized modules. The architecture organizes problem-solving around goal-directed behavior using problem spaces, states, and operators, with a universal subgoaling mechanism for impasses. A key innovation is the chunking mechanism for learning from experience, which automatically acquires new productions to improve future performance. The paper demonstrates SOAR's capabilities across multiple domains and provides demonstrations of its problem-solving and learning abilities. SOAR embodies hypotheses about the nature of cognitive architecture, particularly the importance of uniform representation and the role of learning in acquiring task-specific knowledge.

## Key Contributions

- Unified cognitive architecture with minimal set of mechanisms
- Universal subgoaling for handling knowledge limitations
- Chunking mechanism for automatic learning from experience
- Demonstrations across multiple domains showing generality
- Foundation for decades of cognitive architecture research

## Relevance to Thesis

This paper is essential for Section 2.3 (Memory Architectures), providing the historical foundation and theoretical basis for cognitive architectures in AI systems. SOAR's chunking mechanism directly relates to memory and learning in agents.
